Takeaway: ads must look like content that people want to consume. Solve four problems: where to advertise, who sees it, what it says, and how to get permission to contact them.

## 1. Where: pick the platform
True on all four: I have used it and got value as a consumer, I can target people interested in my stuff, I know how to format for it, there is a minimum spend to place an ad. Pro tip: start where your competitors advertise and copy placements. Action: watch ads on one platform, use each platform's ad library, write down the top 50 ads (hook, retain, reward, offer), write 10 versions of your own, choose 3. Leo's note: Substack?

## 2. Who: select the audience (hardest part)
- Cut down, be specific: puddle, then pond.
- Lookalike: upload warm and cold lists, a bigger and better list means better targeting.
- Target a factor: age, income, gender, interest, time, location. Leo's open items: "Men??", "2 filters max??".
- Local ads: markets are tiny, be specific but not more, about 2 filters max.
- Action: put lists in one place, split past and present customers, warm and cold. Keep the list of people who engaged with ads but did not buy. Use lists in order of quality for the lookalike, then add filters.

## 3. What: the ad
Components: callout (first impression is the most important part; the headline is the first sale, 80 cents of each ad dollar), value (model the value-equation language), CTA (tell them what to do next).
Callout types: labels ("Are you a homeowner"), yes-questions ("Do you sweat at night"), if-then ("If you were born between X and Y you may qualify"), ridiculous results (bizarre, rare), and sensory: contrast (colors, sound, movement), likeness (show their label and traits; to sell to doctors wear a lab coat; like them but slightly better), scene (a local landmark). Study ads, do not skip them.
Ideation framework What x Who x When:
- What, more: dream, fast, easy, likely. What, less: nightmare, slow, hard, risky.
- Who: themselves, family, friends, colleagues, rivals.
- When: past, present, future (good and bad).
Multiplicative: 8 reasons x 5 avatars x 3 timelines. Make ads as specific as you can, but no more.

## 4. Permission to contact
Make the CTA specific ("Click here", "Reply YES", short domains). Simple landing page: headline, subheadline, (image), email, submit button, (benefits). Landing page must match the ad. Leo's action: build my landing page.
