People remember an experience by its most intense moment and its ending, not by the average. Patients who remember the clinic well come back, refer and leave good reviews, so fix the worst moment and the ending first.

## Steps
1. Map the patient journey: WhatsApp inquiry, booking, reminder, arrival, visit, payment, follow-up.
2. Mark the most intense moments, good and bad (likely bad: the price conversation, a long wait).
3. Mark the ending (payment and goodbye) and what comes right after.
4. Fix the worst moment first, then make the ending warm and clear.
5. Plan a recovery for failures (apology plus a small gesture); a good recovery can outweigh the failure.

## What we control
First-reply speed and the post-visit message: see [[Post-visit follow-up message]]. A paid audit that finds the worst moment and the ending: [[Patient journey audit offer]].

## Evidence in one line
Cold-water study: people chose to repeat a longer trial because it ended milder.

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
Interviews, restaurants, concerts and support after an outage all work the same way: judges compress long evidence into a few vivid samples, so control the samples.

## Limits
Summaries only; the 1993 paper was not read. Sample sizes, replication and where the effect fails: `unknown`. Examples on the source pages are company claims, not controlled tests.
