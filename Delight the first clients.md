Takeaway: with the first clinics, go far beyond reasonable service. The product can be unfinished; the experience of being our client must be excellent.

## Graham's points
- Wufoo mailed each new user a handwritten thank-you note for as long as it could.
- Graham has never seen a startup led into a dead end by trying too hard to please its first users.
- Founders resist because: engineers are not trained in service, it "won't scale", and they only know big-company service. His answers: you have nothing to lose yet, and the habit spreads through the culture.
- "Insanely great" early means the experience of being the user, not the software. Attention fills the gaps in an early product.
- Do not copy big companies' indifference to look "professional."

## What we do
1. Weekly personal message to each pilot clinic with its numbers: messages in, response time, consultations booked. A voice note from Leo works in WhatsApp.
2. A thank-you when the first patient from our system walks in, sent to the owner and the receptionist.
3. A named person answers any clinic question the same day.
4. Use the peak-end rule: make the worst moment and the ending of each month excellent ([[Peak-end rule]], [[Post-visit follow-up message]]).
5. Ask for a short quote at the end of the pilot, in the owner's words, with permission to use it ([[Pilot success criteria from the owner's answer]]).

## Example (draft, Spanish to be reviewed)
"Doctora, esta semana entraron 34 mensajes, respondimos en promedio en 4 minutos y se agendaron 6 consultas. Una paciente preguntó por el precio a las 11 pm y ya quedó con cita el jueves." The numbers here are an illustration only, not real results.

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
A new restaurant treats its first 50 guests as regulars; a coach spends extra time on the first recruits. Early believers set the culture and spread the word, so their experience is worth more than their payment.

## Limits
Graham's view comes from software startups. How much time per clinic this takes is `unknown`; stop doing it by hand only when it blocks the next clinic ([[Do things that don't scale]]).
